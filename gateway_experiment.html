Limitations observed

  • It invents citations. It gave page 71 for the offside offence (actually pp. 109–111), and quoted wording from the 2023/24 Laws that isn't in them.
  • It sounds equally sure when wrong. The wrong answers are as confident and well formatted as the right ones, so a referee can't tell them apart.

This is the case for the rest of the project: answers must be grounded in retrieved sections of the current Laws, with citations taken from laws_chunks and its original page numbers, not from the model's memory.

What is the minimum distance opponents must be from the ball at a free kick?

Correct
Model answer
The opponents must stand **at least 9.15 m (10 yd)** away from the ball at a free kick.

**Reference:** IFAB Law 13 – *Free Kicks* (the “minimum distance” requirement for defending players).
What the 2026/27 Laws say

Law 13: opponents at least 9.15 m (10 yds) from the ball.

2

A defender deliberately handles the ball on the goal line to stop a certain goal. What is the decision?

Correct
Model answer
**Decision:** Award a penalty kick and send the defender off (red card).

**Citations**

- **Law 14 – The Penalty Kick** – A penalty kick is awarded if a player commits a direct free‑kick offence (including deliberate handling of the ball) inside his own penalty area.
- **Law 12 – Fouls and Misconduct** – Deliberate handling of the ball to deny an opponent a goal‑scoring opportunity is a *denying an obvious goal‑scoring opportunity* (DOGSO) offence, which warrants a **red card** (sending‑off).

So the referee should:
1. Award a penalty kick to the attacking team.
2. Show the defender a red card for DOGSO (deliberate handball).
What the 2026/27 Laws say

Law 12.4, DOGSO (p124): a player who denies a goal by a deliberate handball offence is sent off; Law 14 gives the penalty kick.

3

Can a player be penalised for offside if they receive the ball directly from a throw-in?

Right answer, wrong source
Model answer
**No.** A player cannot be penalised for off‑side when he receives the ball directly from a throw‑in.

**Relevant rule:**
- **Law 11 – Offside**, paragraph *Offside offence* (2023/24 Laws): “A player is not in an off‑side position if he receives the ball directly from a goal kick, a corner kick, or a throw‑in.”

Therefore, if the ball is thrown in and a teammate catches or otherwise plays it immediately, the off‑side rule does not apply.
What the 2026/27 Laws say

Law 11.3 No offence (p111): “There is no offside offence if a player receives the ball directly from: a goal kick, a throw-in, a corner kick.” The model quotes wording that isn't in the Laws and cites the 2023/24 edition.

4

Under the 2026/27 Laws, what happens if a goalkeeper holds the ball in their hands for too long?

Wrong
Model answer
**What the Law says**

- **Law 12 – Fouls and Misconduct** (2026/27 edition) states that a goalkeeper is allowed to control the ball with his/her hands for **no more than 6 seconds** after it has been released from a stoppage (e.g., a goal‑kick, corner, throw‑in, free kick, or a deliberate pass from a teammate).
- If the goalkeeper **holds, carries or touches the ball with his/her hands for longer than 6 seconds**, the referee awards an **indirect free kick** to the opposing team.

**Where the restart is taken**

- The indirect free kick is taken **from the place where the offence occurred**.
- If the offence occurs inside the goalkeeper’s own penalty area, the indirect free kick is taken from the spot of the offence, **but not closer than the edge of the penalty area** (i.e., the kick is taken from the nearest point on the line of the penalty area).

**Summary**

- **Too‑long hold (>6 s) → indirect free kick to the opponents** (Law 12).
What the 2026/27 Laws say

Law 12.3 Corner kick (p119): a corner kick is awarded if a goalkeeper controls the ball with their hands for more than eight seconds. The model gives the old rule (six seconds, indirect free kick).
